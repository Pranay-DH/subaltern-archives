Counter-Archives of the Unheard:
Gender, Labor, and Sacred Sexuality
in South Asia & Beyond

"Historical canons have long silenced subaltern voices through moral panic, state surveillance, and colonial erasure. This archive exists to restore epistemic dignity to those who lived, labored, and spoke on the margins."

Why This Archive Matters

For centuries, official state gazettes, colonial penal codes, and reformist tracts have categorized sex workers, temple performers (*Devadasi/Jogti*), courtesans (*Tawaifs*), and gender-nonconforming communities as passive subjects of social rescue, criminal surveillance, or moral degradation.

This counter-archive reverses that historical violence by re-centering subaltern individuals as active theorists and historians of their own lived realities.

Epistemic Dignity & Anti-Canonical Praxis

By digitizing unedited first-person memoirs, trade union manifestos, 78 RPM shellac recordings, and secret street argots (*Khechuri*), the archive preserves the technology of subaltern resistance in its original linguistic and emotional context.

Spanning over a century (1919–2023) alongside classical antiquity records, it serves scholars, students, and activists seeking non-canonical knowledge frameworks rooted in bodily autonomy and labor rights.

Methodological Framework

Anti-Canonical Archival Praxis

1. Subaltern Historiography: Replaces top-down colonial police records and moral reformist pamphlets with unedited testimonies, oral tape recordings, and self-published autobiographies.

2. Linguistic Preservation: Catalogs non-canonical subaltern argots (such as Bengal's Khechuri secret sociolect) to preserve the technology of linguistic resistance.

3. Standardized Archival Metadata: Every item is indexed using standard catalog elements (Title, Creator, Subject, Rights, Source) ensuring global academic interoperability.
